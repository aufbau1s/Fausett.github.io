Reading

Books, papers, courses, and lectures I'm working through or want to return to.

Applied math

  • Linear Algebra Done Right Sheldon Axler

    The right way to learn linear algebra if you care about structure over computation. Builds everything from operators rather than matrices.

  • A Mathematical Introduction to Compressive Sensing Foucart & Rauhut

    Working through the linear algebra foundations. Dense but worth it.

  • Numerical Analysis Burden & Faires

    Standard numerical methods reference. Useful alongside coursework.

Machine learning

  • Deep Learning Goodfellow, Bengio, Courville

    Reference more than a read-through. Useful for the mathematical foundations sections.

  • Understanding Deep Learning Simon Prince

    More accessible than Goodfellow for building intuition. Good figures.

Mechanistic interpretability

  • In-context Learning and Induction Heads Olsson et al., Anthropic (2022)

    The clearest example I've found of mechanistic interpretability doing something concrete and verifiable.

  • Toy Models of Superposition Elhage et al., Anthropic (2022)

    Why models represent more features than they have dimensions. The geometry is interesting and the math is tractable.

  • Feature Visualization Olah et al., Distill (2017)

    Good starting point for understanding what early interpretability work was trying to do.

Business & operations

  • High Output Management Andy Grove

    The best book on running operations I've read. Grove treats management as an engineering problem.

  • The Goal Eliyahu Goldratt

    Theory of constraints applied to manufacturing. More useful for thinking about bottlenecks than most business books.

  • Thinking in Systems Donella Meadows

    Useful framework for understanding why businesses behave the way they do at scale.

Writing & other

  • On Writing Well William Zinsser

    The standard reference for clear nonfiction writing. Worth rereading.

  • The Elements of Style Strunk & White

    Short and useful. Keep it nearby.
