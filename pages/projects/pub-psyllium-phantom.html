Tissue Mimicking Phantom for Ultrasound & MRI

Gelatin phantoms are standard for MRI research but nearly invisible to ultrasound because they don't scatter sound the way real tissue does. This paper characterizes a simple fix: adding psyllium husk to the gelatin matrix. The goal was a phantom that works for both modalities simultaneously, which matters for comparing MR-guided and ultrasound-guided HIFU as treatment options. Psyllium husk had been used in low-resource clinical training settings, and the key questions were whether it would introduce too much MRI noise or create air pockets that would corrupt either modality. It didn't. Across nine concentrations, acoustic properties stayed nearly constant while echogenicity scaled predictably with concentration, giving researchers a tunable, inexpensive dual-modality phantom.

I joined the lab at UCAIR as a freshman doing cold outreach to research groups. I designed the experimental protocol, mixed and tested the phantoms, collected the ultrasound data, wrote the MATLAB analysis pipeline from scratch, and drafted the full manuscript (Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion) with the exception of the MRI-specific sections. The lab director was out for a significant stretch during the writing phase, so the manuscript was largely self-directed. I left the university before the paper was published, but the work was complete.

Development and Characterization of a Tissue Mimicking Psyllium Husk Gelatin Phantom for Ultrasound and Magnetic Resonance Imaging
