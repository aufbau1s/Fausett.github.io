About

Ten years ago, I dropped out of the University of Utah, leaving behind the exciting world of medical physics to become an executive in e-commerce.

My main focus for the last decade was Patrick Adair Designs. My high school friend Patrick started the company in his mom's basement at 18, and we built it into a real business. Through that process I got to do work I'm still proud of: we grew a YouTube channel to over 800,000 subscribers, and I produced and edited the videos behind its first 51 million views and 3,265,000 hours of watch time. That is approximately four lifetimes of viewing, if one human did it all.

As CEO and acting CMO, I sold nearly nine figures in jewelry and spent over $20M of our own money on advertising. I also oversaw our manufacturing climb from a garage, to our first US-based shop, to a factory we built ourselves in China.

Unfortunately, not everything is made to last.

Related reading: Your business isn't a golden goose and Why incentives determine outcomes, both in the pipeline.

Since I left, my goal has been to build as deep and rigorous an understanding of mathematics as I can. Candidly, this was largely driven by listening to marketers argue about optimization, and realizing we are all imbeciles who know nothing about real math or data science.

I originally returned to the University of Utah, but my wife has always dreamed of living in New York, and we figured there was no time like the present. So I transferred to Columbia University to study Applied Mathematics.

When I'm not studying, you can find me at home with my three dogs, or consulting with companies in the performance marketing and consumer space.
