Publication

Development and Characterization of a Tissue Mimicking Psyllium Husk Gelatin Phantom for Ultrasound and Magnetic Resonance Imaging

International Journal of Hyperthermia · Vol. 37, No. 1 · 2020 · doi:10.1080/02656736.2020.1739345  ·  PubMed

Hofstetter LW, Fausett L, Mueller A, Odéen H, Payne A, Christensen DA, Parker DL. My contribution: experimental execution, ultrasound data collection, MATLAB analysis, and manuscript writing.

abstract

Purpose: To develop and characterize a tissue-mimicking phantom suitable for both ultrasound (US) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) using psyllium husk as an ultrasound scatterer in a gelatin matrix. Methods: Nine psyllium husk concentrations (0–4% w/v) were tested across four ultrasound frequencies. MRI relaxometry (T1, T2) and acoustic property measurements (speed of sound, attenuation) were performed. Results: Echogenicity increased with psyllium concentration while MRI quality degraded at higher concentrations. Acoustic properties remained consistent across all concentrations. A 1–2% psyllium concentration provided an effective balance for dual-modality imaging. Conclusion: Psyllium husk gelatin phantoms offer a low-cost, reproducible option for combined US/MRI research applications, including HIFU treatment planning and validation.
