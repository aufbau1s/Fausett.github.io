About

Ten years ago, I dropped out of the University of Utah, leaving behind the exciting world of medical physics to become an executive in e-commerce.

My main focus for the last decade was Patrick Adair Designs. My high school friend Patrick started the company in his mom's basement at 18, and we spent the better part of a decade building it into a real business. As I transitioned from co-founder #2, to marketing guy, to operations guy, and finally to CEO, I got to work on incredible projects.

I got to live every Gen Alpha's dream and grow a YouTube channel to 800k+ subscribers, working directly as the producer and editor of the first 51M views.

51M views ⇝ 3,265,000 hours = 372 years ≈ 4.7 lifetimes
If you pressed play in 1654, you'd be a third of the way through by the time America declared independence.

Built manufacturing facilities from a workshop in his landlord's garage, to our own building in downtown Salt Lake, to eventually building our own factory in China.

Personally managed and spent over $20M in advertising on performance platforms.

Distributed eight figures of cash flow to our investors and partners.

Unfortunately, not everything is made to last.

Two essays on why: Your business isn't a golden goose and Why incentives determine outcomes, both in the pipeline.

Since I left, my goal has been to collect experiences and knowledge to further push the hungering maw of my curiosity. This led me to studying Applied Mathematics at Columbia University, driven by a love of optimization, modeling, and data science (and more recently, analysis).

When I'm not studying, you can find me at home with my three dogs, or consulting with companies in the performance marketing and consumer space.
