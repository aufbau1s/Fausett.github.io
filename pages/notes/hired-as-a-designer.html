How to get hired as a designer when you have no portfolio

I've reviewed 348 design applicants in one morning. Less than 10% made it past the resume stage. If you're trying to get a job as a creative, the bar is surprisingly low — here's how to clear it by a mile.

Step 1: Get your portfolio online.
This is the easiest screening step to fail. If a company asks for your portfolio and you can’t send one, you’ll be auto-rejected. Even if they don’t ask, not having it linked on your résumé means you’re unlikely to be hired.

No professional portfolio yet?
Design is one of the rare fields where you can “work for free” for yourself to build experience.

Here’s how I’d build a portfolio from scratch:

  1. Sketch one design: Use a $20 iPhone tripod from Amazon to film the process.
  2. Model it: In Rhino3D (or your preferred software), screen-record yourself creating the piece.
  3. Get it made: Use a casting shop like Carrera (NYC). If you prefer to avoid phone calls and account setup, Shapeways is easier but pricier.
  4. Finish in an affordable metal: Brass or copper works fine.
  5. Photograph the journey: Include the sketch, a render of the model, and a photo of the prototype in your portfolio.

Want to go from better than 90% of applicants to better than 99.9%?
Turn the process into short-form content:

  • Sketch phase video: Time-lapse your drawing with a simple narrated hook like “Come with me to design X.” A unique angle is even better.
  • Modeling video: Speed-ramped footage of you building it in 3D.
  • Finished piece video: Showcase the final result.
  • Compilation video: Edit all three phases into one.

Each should be 20–60 seconds (shorter is usually better). Edit in CapCut.

Put a dedicated work contact email in your bio, and post at least once a week for a year. More is better.
